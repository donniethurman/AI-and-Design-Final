That's Cheating!

A design conversation about AI, authorship, and direction.

Designer Against

Well...AI slop.

Designer For

I get why it feels that way.
But I think people are talking about AI as if it’s one thing. In practice it shows up in three very different ways in design.
First as a tool, speeding up small tasks. Second, as a collaborator, where constraints and prompts shape the output. And third, meta-creation, where the designer’s role shifts toward directing and refining what the system produces.
The interesting question isn’t whether AI is good or bad. It’s where the designer sits in that process.

Designer A

Easy for you to say.
People can generate posters, images, even websites in seconds now.
I spent years learning those skills. Sketching. Typography. Composition.
Now someone types a sentence and suddenly they’re a “designer”?

Designer For

That frustration is completely fair.
But collaboration in design isn’t new.
Designers already work with photographers, developers, printers, motion artists.
AI just shifts the boundary of who — or what — is involved.
In some ways it democratizes access to tools that used to require a whole team. Which means the designer’s value shifts a little...from pure execution toward direction and judgment.

Designer Against

But that “creative abundance” argument bothers me.
There's actually a paradox there.
Creativity gets overwhelmed when it has access to too many resources and if everyone can generate endless images…doesn’t that just flood the world with mediocre work?

Designer For

It absolutely can.
Abundance without intention usually produces noise. But that’s where designers still matter.
The role becomes curation.
Choosing what’s worth keeping.
Imposing constraints. Shaping the system so the output actually means something. Automation can produce quantity.
Design still decides quality.

Designer Against

Okay, but here’s the part that actually worries me.
Cultural homogenization.
These systems are trained on the same giant pool of data.
Doesn’t that just push everything toward the same visual language?

Designer For

That risk is real.
But it’s also where thoughtful designers can intervene.
If you treat AI as an autopilot, you’ll get generic results. If you treat it like a material, something you experiment with and constrain…
You can push it in unexpected directions.
Pacing is where we address those concerns.
Which is when a designer checks AI work at every step of the process, to ensure it is up to the standards you impose. Such as accessibility and cultural representation.

Maybe the real shift isn’t that designers are becoming less important.
It’s that our role is changing.
Human judgment, taste, and intention might actually matter more now than before.
Change is inevitable.
The shape of that change is up to us.
The technology isn’t the final author.
The designer still decides what survives
